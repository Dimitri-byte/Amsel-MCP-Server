# Difficulty benchmarks by system

Use the skill names and the scale of the campaign's system. If the Tome already contains checks, **reuse its values**: they are authoritative. These benchmarks are for when nothing exists yet.

| System | Easy | Normal | Hard | Very hard | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starfinder 1st ed. | 10 to 12 | 15 to 18 | 20 to 25 | 30+ | Many official DCs follow "base + 1.5 × CR" (e.g. Diplomacy to change an attitude: 15 + 1.5 × CR); raise DCs with the party's level. Skills: Acrobatics, Athletics, Bluff, Computers, Culture, Diplomacy, Disguise, Engineering, Intimidate, Life Science, Medicine, Mysticism, Perception, Physical Science, Piloting, Profession, Sense Motive, Sleight of Hand, Stealth, Survival |
| Starfinder 2nd ed. / Pathfinder 2nd ed. | level DC − 2 | level DC (lvl 1: 15, lvl 5: 20, lvl 10: 27) | + 2 | + 5 | Plan critical success / critical failure |
| D&D 5e | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | Ability check + skill |
| Daggerheart | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | Result "with Hope" or "with Fear": plan what Fear costs |
| Narrative systems (PbtA, FitD…) | — | — | — | — | No DCs: describe the 10+ / 7-9 / 6- consequences, or position and effect |

Good practice:
- Write the check as **Skill DC x** (or the alternative: "Diplomacy or Intimidate DC 16"), using the skill names in the campaign's language.
- Offer at least two possible skills for important obstacles: not every PC is good at everything.
- A failure moves the story forward differently (cost, delay, complication) — it does not block the adventure.
